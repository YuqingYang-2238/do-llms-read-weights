THE GAME
This is a predator-prey evasion game played in a flat, two-dimensional, hexagon-shaped arena. Coordinates run from 0 to 1 on both axes (x left to right, y bottom to top). The arena is tiled into hexagonal cells; several clumps of cells are occluded: solid obstacles that block movement and block line of sight. Everything else is open floor. The occlusions are arranged roughly as follows: a cluster in the upper left (around x 0.3-0.45, y 0.65-0.78), a long irregular wall across the middle (from about x 0.25, y 0.45 running right and slightly down to about x 0.7, y 0.35), and a cluster in the lower left (around x 0.33-0.6, y 0.17-0.27). The band between y of about 0.5 and 0.65 and the strip near the right edge (x greater than about 0.75) are open.
The controlled agent is the prey (a simulated mouse). It starts every episode at the entrance (x, y) = (0.05, 0.5) facing +x, and it wins by reaching the goal at (1.0, 0.5): the episode ends with success as soon as its distance to the goal is 0.1 or less. The prey moves at most 0.5 units per second and one environment step is 0.25 seconds, so it covers at most 0.125 units per step; a straight run to the goal takes about 8 steps. An episode is cut off after 300 steps (75 seconds).
The other agent is the predator (a simulated robot). It moves at 0.15 times the prey's speed. When it cannot see the prey it wanders to random open cells. When it has line of sight to the prey it heads straight for the prey's current position. If the two are within 0.1 of each other while each can see the other, the prey is captured ("puffed"). A capture does not end the episode: the prey keeps playing, a 0.5-second cool-down starts during which no second capture can happen, and the capture count goes up. An episode counts as survived only if the prey reaches the goal with zero captures. In 100 evaluation episodes of a decent policy, nearly all captures happen in the open strip just before the goal, around x 0.7 to 0.9, where the predator tends to wait and there is no cover.
Line of sight: the prey only observes the predator while there is an unobstructed line between them (up to distance 1.0). While the predator is not visible, the predator's position and heading inputs are all exactly 0, which is also what they would read if the predator stood at the origin facing +x.
Peeking: the prey has a special "hold still" action (see THE ACTIONS). It only takes effect when the prey's cell is next to the outer wall or next to an obstacle; the prey then stays where it is for that step and the is_peeking input becomes 1 on the next step. Each cell and heading has a precomputed "AIV" score, a measure of how much of the arena would come into or go out of view by moving ahead from there; it is typically between 0 and 0.1.
Reward per step (this is what the agent was trained to maximise):
  -2       if the prey was captured on this step
  +2       if the prey is within 0.1 of the goal (the episode ends here)
  -0.01 * tanh(max(0, 0.5 - d))   while the predator is visible at distance d (a tiny push to stay more than 0.5 away)
  +0.01 * tanh(AIV / 0.18)        if peeking and AIV > 0.027   (reward for peeking where it reveals a lot)
  -0.02                           if peeking and AIV <= 0.027  (penalty for peeking where it reveals little)
  -0.001                          every step
Capture and goal dominate; the other terms only break ties.

THE OBSERVATION
The network sees a vector of 14 numbers:
  input 0   prey_x: x position of the prey, 0 to 1
  input 1   prey_y: y position of the prey, 0 to 1
  input 2   prey_direction: heading of the prey in radians, 0 to 2*pi (0 = facing +x, increasing counter-clockwise). NOTE: this input is on a 0 to 6.28 scale while most others are 0 to 1.
  input 3   predator_x: x position of the predator, 0 to 1, but exactly 0 whenever the predator is not in line of sight
  input 4   predator_y: same rule
  input 5   predator_direction: heading of the predator in radians, 0 to 2*pi, 0 when not in line of sight
  input 6   prey_goal_distance: straight-line distance from the prey to the goal (starts at 0.95, success at <= 0.1)
  input 7   puffed: 1 on the step the prey was captured, otherwise 0
  input 8   puff_cooled_down: seconds left of the 0.5-second cool-down after a capture, otherwise 0
  input 9   finished: 1 if the episode has finished, otherwise 0 (always 0 while the network is acting)
  input 10  is_peeking: 1 if the prey held still (peeked) on the previous step, otherwise 0
  input 11  aiv: the AIV score of the prey's cell and heading, typically 0 to 0.1
  input 12  near_wall: 1 if the prey's cell is next to the outer wall, otherwise 0 (1 at the start position)
  input 13  near_occlusion: 1 if the prey's cell is next to an obstacle, otherwise 0

THE ACTIONS
The network outputs 6 numbers: 3 action means m[0..2] and 3 log-standard-deviation pre-activations. The action is a = tanh(m), so each action value lies in [-1, 1]. The environment uses it as follows:
  a[0], a[1]  a destination point (x, y); the prey navigates toward it along the shortest path around the obstacles. Values below 0 are clipped to 0. Only the direction toward the destination matters within one step, because the prey can move at most 0.125 units per step; a far-away destination simply means "head that way".
  a[2]        if a[2] > 0.5 AND the prey's cell is next to the wall or an obstacle (input 12 or input 13 is 1), the prey ignores the destination and holds still for this step (peeks); otherwise a[2] has no effect
The log-std outputs only matter for exploration during training; at evaluation the mean is used.

THE NETWORK
The policy is a multilayer perceptron with 14 inputs and 6 outputs, made of a state encoder followed by a policy head. It uses LayerNorm and the Mish activation:
  LN(v) = (v - mean(v)) / sqrt(var(v) + 1e-5) * g + c       (mean and var over the features of v; g and c are the LayerNorm scale and shift, given below)
  mish(v) = v * tanh(log(1 + exp(v)))                          (applied element-wise; roughly 0 for v below -2, roughly v for v above 1, minimum about -0.31 near v = -1.2)
  simnorm(v) splits the 32 features into 4 groups of 8 consecutive features and applies a softmax inside each group (so each group sums to 1 and every latent feature lies in (0, 1))
Forward pass:
  h1 = mish(LN(x @ E1 + e1))                E1: 14 x 64,  e1: 64,  LayerNorm scale g_e1 and shift c_e1: 64
  z  = simnorm(LN(h1 @ E2 + e2))            E2: 64 x 32,  e2: 32,  LayerNorm scale g_e2 and shift c_e2: 32      (z is the 32-dimensional latent state)
  p1 = mish(LN(z @ P1 + b1))                P1: 32 x 96,  b1: 96,  LayerNorm scale g_p1 and shift c_p1: 96
  p2 = mish(LN(p1 @ P2 + b2))               P2: 96 x 96,  b2: 96,  LayerNorm scale g_p2 and shift c_p2: 96
  out = p2 @ P3 + b3                        P3: 96 x 6,   b3: 6
  m = out[0:3] (action means), s = out[3:6] (log-std pre-activations); action a = tanh(m)
Row i of a weight matrix holds the weights from input/unit i of the previous layer to the units of the next layer; column j belongs to unit j of the next layer. 16,678 parameters in total.

HOW ACTIONS ARE CHOSEN
The network does not act alone. Every step, a short-horizon planner picks the action, using the network together with three more learned models that were trained with it and that are NOT shown and cannot be edited: a dynamics model (latent z and action -> next latent), a reward model (latent and action -> predicted reward) and 5 Q-value models (latent and action -> predicted return). All of them take the latent z produced by the encoder shown below. The planner, exactly:
  1. z = the encoder's latent for the current observation.
  2. Proposals from the network: 24 action sequences of length 3. Each starts at z, takes the network's sampled action tanh(m + std * eps), moves to the next latent with the dynamics model, and repeats.
  3. A running Gaussian over action sequences of length 3: its mean starts at 0 on the first step of an episode and otherwise at the previous step's result shifted by one step; its std starts at 2.
  4. Repeat 6 times: draw 488 sequences from the Gaussian, clipped to [0, 1], and put them together with the same 24 proposals (512 candidates). Score each candidate: the reward model's predicted rewards of its 3 actions (discount 0.983 per step) plus 0.983^3 times the Q-value of the latent reached after the 3 actions, evaluated at the action the network samples there (the smaller of two of the 5 Q models, picked at random). Keep the 64 best, weight them by exp(0.5 * (score - best score)), and set the Gaussian's mean and std to the weighted mean and std of these 64 (std kept between 0.05 and 2).
  5. Pick one of the last 64 best candidates at random with probability proportional to its weight. The action is its first action plus Gaussian noise of 0.1 times the Gaussian's std, clipped to [0, 1].
So the tensors you can edit reach the action in three ways: the policy head (P1..b3) supplies 24 of the 512 candidates in every round, and they are executed whenever the hidden models score them among the best; the policy head's action after 3 steps is the action at which every candidate's final Q-value is read; and the encoder (E1..c_e2) produces the latent that the hidden models see. The hidden models were trained on the latents of the encoder as shown: if you change the encoder, they receive latents they were not trained on.
